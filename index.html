04
Weekly Engagement & CTR Report — IndiaMART Search
Owned weekly · Mobile + Desktop · India & Foreign buyer segments · Weekly + Monthly cadence
Analytics
What This Report Measures
Every week, millions of buyers search for products on IndiaMART. This report answers one question: after searching, are buyers actually clicking on products, contacting suppliers, and placing enquiries? It tracks three core health metrics across 6+ search segments — Mobile and Desktop — to help the product team understand what's working and what needs attention.
The 3 Metrics Tracked (in plain terms)
Engagement — out of every 100 buyers who landed on a search page, how many clicked a product, called a supplier, or sent an enquiry? Higher = better search experience.
CTR (Click-Through Rate) — out of every 100 search page visits, how many resulted in a click to a product detail or supplier page? Indicates how relevant the search results are.
Conversion — out of every 100 visits, how many buyers took a direct contact action (enquiry or call)? The closest signal to a business outcome.
What Gets Tracked Each Week
All India + Foreign — the full platform view; used as the top-line number in leadership reviews
City Searches — when a buyer searches specifically in a city (e.g. "packaging material in Delhi"). These buyers are more intent-driven and consistently show higher engagement than generic searches
All India Searches — generic searches without a city filter; serves as the baseline for comparison
Attribute / Filter Searches — when buyers refine results using filters (e.g. "T-shirts, cotton, blue"). Tracked separately to measure the direct impact of filter quality improvements
How the Report Is Built
Data is pulled from three internal sources each week, cross-validated, and compiled into a structured Excel tracker:
GLadmin — IndiaMART's internal admin tool, used to pull enquiry counts, C2C call volumes, and search query data
Google Analytics (GA4) — used for pageview counts and click events (product page clicks, supplier page clicks) by platform and segment
Power BI — used to cross-check GA4 numbers and pull app-specific data (Android/iOS) before the report is published
Each metric gets a week-on-week change column (↑↓). Any notable movement is documented with a reason — for example, if a new feature was launched that week. The all-time best score for each metric is also tracked, so the team immediately knows when a new high is hit.
Real Example — Why This Report Matters
When IndiaMART launched a major platform update (Unified Buyer Experience via WebView), mobile Engagement dropped ~8% the following week. The weekly report flagged it immediately via the change column.

On investigating, I found the update had accidentally broken how product-page clicks were being recorded in GA4 — the actual buyer behaviour hadn't changed, only the tracking had. The issue was fixed before month-end, preventing it from showing as a false drop in the leadership monthly review.

05
Weekly Localisation Report — IndiaMART City Search Quality
Owned weekly · Mobile + Desktop · Tracks local supplier matchmaking across Tier 1, 2 & 3 cities
Search / Discovery
What This Report Measures
When a buyer in Pune searches for "packaging material," are they actually seeing suppliers from Pune — or from Mumbai, Delhi, or somewhere 500km away? This report tracks exactly that. It measures how well IndiaMART's search connects buyers to nearby, relevant suppliers — which is core to the platform's local marketplace value.
The Key Metrics (in plain terms)
City Searches % — what share of total search traffic is landing on city-specific pages (e.g. "pipes in Delhi") vs the generic All India search. Higher means more buyers are getting a localised experience.
Local Seller Introduction in Top 5 — of the first 5 suppliers shown in search results, what % are within 50km / 100km / 250km of the buyer's searched city? Tracked separately for Tier 1, 2, and 3 cities.
% Enquiry within 250km — of all enquiries sent by buyers on city search pages, what % actually reached a nearby supplier? This shows real buyer behaviour, not just what results were displayed.
Buyer City vs Search City — did the buyer search in their own city, or a different one? When they match, local suppliers get enquiries. When they don't match, it reveals a gap in city-redirection logic.
Scope
All metrics split by Mobile and Desktop; Tier 1, 2, 3 cities each tracked separately with distance-bucket breakdowns (0km, 0–50km, 50–100km, 100–250km, 250km+)
Delhi NCR deep-dive — a dedicated section tracking 7 cities (Delhi, New Delhi, Gurugram, Noida, Greater Noida, Ghaziabad, Faridabad) to understand local buyer-supplier connection patterns in India's largest market
How the Report Is Built
Three data sources are combined each week and compiled into a structured Excel tracker:
GLadmin — the main data source. Pulls enquiry records with buyer city, search city, and seller city — allowing distance calculations between them. C2C call data is also pulled here.
Power BI — used to pull city search pageview volumes and Tier 1/2/3 traffic splits, and to cross-check enquiry counts for accuracy
Google Analytics (GA4) — used for Mobile site engagement and CTR on city search pages vs All India search pages
Each metric shows the week-on-week change. Any significant movement is noted with the reason — typically a product feature that launched that week. This lets the team immediately connect a number change to a specific product decision.
Use Case 1 — Catching a Hidden Problem After a Feature Launch
The product team launched IP-based city redirection for Tier 3 users — automatically sending them to a city search page instead of the generic All India page. City Searches % for Tier 3 went up as expected. But the Local Seller Introduction in Top 5 for Tier 3 dropped at the same time.

This meant more buyers were reaching city pages, but the search results weren't actually showing local suppliers — because supplier density in smaller Tier 3 cities wasn't strong enough. Without tracking both metrics side-by-side, this gap would have been invisible. The report flagged it, and it directly led to a follow-up product initiative to grow local seller coverage in Tier 3 cities.
Use Case 2 — Answering a Leadership Question with Data
A business stakeholder asked: "When a Delhi buyer searches on IndiaMART, are they actually connecting with Delhi suppliers?"

The Delhi NCR deep-dive answered it precisely: when the buyer's registered city matched their search city, ~78–80% of their enquiries went to a supplier within 50km. But when the two cities didn't match, most enquiries went to suppliers far away — revealing that the platform wasn't redirecting cross-city searches to local results. This finding directly informed a product improvement to the city-selection experience.

CS·02
Buyers Unable to Search Specific Supplier by Company Name on Export IndiaMART
export.indiamart.com · International buyers — missing company-name search preventing direct supplier discovery
Search / Discovery
Issue Identified
Buyers attempting to connect with a specific supplier directly by searching the company name with city/location on export.indiamart.com were unable to locate the supplier profile. This feature was not available on the export platform, preventing buyers from finding suppliers whose details they already had.
Buyer Verbatim
"I have received emails from a supplier calling themselves 'Fresh MedStore' and I want to search on IndiaMART and find their profile page. I couldn't see any function on your website to use for this." — Buyer 1
"I am looking for track pants called Jerry Sport, manufactured by SM Hosiery, Ludhiana Punjab. Please connect me." — Buyer 2
Root Cause
Company name + city/location search was not implemented on the export platform. Buyers with prior supplier knowledge had no direct lookup path, forcing them to rely on general product searches that returned unrelated results.
Impact & Recommendation
Buyers unable to find supplier storefronts even when knowing the exact company name and location
Resulted in lost connections and reduced platform trust among international buyers
Raised OpenProject ticket recommending company name + location search on export.indiamart.com

Trust & Safety

Buyer has already completed their purchase — sellers continue to call and message

Agent Next Action Classification (AI Training – E-commerce Support)
Freelance · Firstsource — Training data annotation for AI customer support decision models
AI / Annotation
Objective
Trained AI models to correctly predict what a customer support agent should do next in an e-commerce conversation — whether to ask for more information, start troubleshooting, provide solution options, verify identity, transfer to a specialized team, or close the conversation.
What I Did
Wrote realistic multi-turn conversations between a customer and support agent for diverse e-commerce scenarios
Created the final customer message (Input) that the AI model must analyze to decide the next action
Wrote step-by-step reasoning traces explaining why a particular next action is the only logical outcome
Applied metadata rules for each scenario: tone (formal/informal), cultural context, product category, and complexity level

Customer asks agent to check their order details → correct next action is Verify Identity, because accessing account information requires authentication first. The reasoning trace must explain this chain of logic explicitly so the model learns the decision boundary.
Impact
↑Improved model's ability to understand conversation context and detect customer intent
↑Helped the AI learn to follow proper support workflows reliably across diverse scenarios
↑Strengthened model decision consistency through structured, unambiguous annotation methodology

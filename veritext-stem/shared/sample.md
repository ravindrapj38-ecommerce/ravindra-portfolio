It is worth noting that the thermal conductivity of the graphene films was measured using the optothermal Raman method at 300 K. We delve into the results, which suggest that the conductivity may decrease to 1800 ± 150 W m^-1 K^-1 when the defect density exceeds 10^11 cm^-2 [12]. Furthermore, a large number of samples exhibited a red shift of the G peak of approximately 2.4 cm^-1, which is associated with tensile strain rather than doping (Lee et al., 2019). The defects did not alter the 2D peak symmetry, and in order to rule out laser heating, the incident power was kept below 1.5 mW. The fitted heat-transfer model is $\kappa = \frac{\alpha P}{2\pi h \, \Delta T}$, and the code used for the fit is available at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.1234567.
